Inside the experiment

Tetrahedra

Each piece is a cage of tetrahedra: a Delaunay mesh of its outline and an inner lattice, extruded into prisms and split three ways. Every tet is pulled back toward its own best-fit rotation of the rest shape (warm-started quaternions) and holds its volume, so a stretched neck thins instead of growing. Rind and pith tets are stiffer. Sixty steps a second, ten substeps each.

Cutting

A piece is a signed-distance grid at 0.01 resolution over the slice's original rest plane, so every piece keeps the zest, pith and segments exactly where they grew. The blade is a vertical plane; carried into each crossed piece through its best-fit rotation it becomes a line. Cutting is a max() of two distance fields, then a closing and an opening with an exact Euclidean distance transform round the new corners — only near the cut, so older edges keep their shape. Connected parts become pieces; crumbs are swept away.

The wavy knife

The crinkle blade is a corrugated plate, A·sin(2π(x − xr)/λ) with A = 0.036 and λ = 0.28. Its cut surface n·(X − o) = A·sin(2π d·(X − o)/λ) lands in rest space as a wavy line, rounded tight (0.026) so the ridges stay crisp. The blade presses straight down and its wedge follows the corrugation. Cut both ways for crinkle cubes.

Cookie cutters

Star, heart, flower and round rings of rolled tin plate. The ring's outline is carried into the clicked piece's rest frame and the piece splits into inside and outside. The thin wall wedges the two apart as it goes down, then the shape pops up and lifts out.

The riding surface

What you see is not the tetrahedra. A finer surface — rounded walls whose top and bottom bevels shrink to fit thin slivers and crinkle ridges, two caps, the membranes and the air bubbles — rides inside the tets by barycentric coordinates, and its normals turn with each tet's deformation. After a cut, the new pieces take their positions and velocities from the old ones the same way.

Light through jelly

A back-face pass measures how much jelly each view ray crosses. The scene behind is refracted by that thickness and filtered by Beer–Lambert absorption; pith scatters milky white, thin edges glow when back-lit, and Fresnel picks up the studio softboxes. Zest, pith, segments and juice vesicles are all shaded from rest-space coordinates, so a fresh cut shows the real inside. Tools are drawn behind the jelly surface, so buried steel is seen through it.
